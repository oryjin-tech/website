FAQ

Payment fee questions,
answered.

Because most of the cost sits outside the contract: card mix shifting to commercial cards, scheme fee revisions, cross-border reclassification, volume tiers that reset, FX surcharges, and more refunds, retries and disputes. Oryjin isolates each driver, prices it in euros and shows it the month it starts. Read the guide →

Often, and rarely by design. Oryjin checks every PSP and acquirer invoice and settlement against your contract terms each month: processing, refund and dispute fees, tier rates and uncontracted lines. Anything billed above contract comes with the payment references your provider can verify.

More than the blended rate shows. Oryjin splits the full cost of each payment into interchange, scheme fees, acquirer markup, FX and per-event fees, by card type, market and currency, and states whether each figure is measured from your provider's data or modelled from published schedules. Read the guide →

Start 60 days before renewal, with a year of your own volume priced against the contract. Oryjin produces the negotiation brief, the asks with the evidence behind each, and a fact sheet you can hand to the provider. Read the guide →

Yes. The forecast is built from your own volume, mix and contract mechanics, with a P10–P90 range and named drivers such as an approaching tier or an announced scheme fee revision. Each closed month is then held against the forecast made at its start. Read the guide →

Watch it weekly, not at month end. Oryjin tracks dispute and chargeback ratios by market and card type against the schemes' thresholds, prices the fees and losses at stake, and alerts the right person in Slack or the inbox when a ratio starts to climb. Read the guide →

All of them. We started with native Stripe and Adyen integrations, and more connectors are on the way. Any other PSP or acquirer is covered today by uploading its settlement reports, invoices and contract terms in the dashboard, which adds data sources and context and makes the reconciliation and the reading of your costs more reliable.

Oryjin is not a PSP and not an orchestrator: it never moves money, never changes a setting, and has no stake in which provider you use. Data is hosted in the EU by Scaleway (Paris), never includes cardholder data, and is covered by an NDA before sharing and a DPA from day one.
